# Care references and scoring

All 47 reference pages below were successfully retrieved and their growing descriptions reviewed on **6 October 2026**. The source is NC State Extension's Plant Toolbox, a university horticultural reference. The RHS and University of Minnesota pages originally considered were not accessible; they are not used as verified references.

## Scope and interpretation

The app is a broad guide for plants grown in pots. Common names can cover different species: the botanical label identifies the intended scope. Generic orchid, ficus, peperomia, hibiscus, mint, tank bromeliad and desert cactus are scoped respectively to Phalaenopsis, Ficus benjamina, Peperomia obtusifolia, Hibiscus rosa-sinensis, Mentha spicata, Guzmania lingulata and Mammillaria. The original 47 entries are retained in their original order. Cacti here exclude forest cacti.

These are **qualitative editorial profiles**, not scientific measurements or watering prescriptions. Source descriptions of dry, moderately dry and moist growing media are simplified into dry throughout, surface/upper medium dry, and lightly moist. A fourth, plant-specific option covers the central reservoir of Guzmania; its roots require sparse watering, not constantly wet soil. Species-specific advice provides the source's nuance. General medium moisture descriptions do not imply a precise drying depth.

Light is simplified into low, bright indirect, some direct sun (2–6 hours), and full sun (6 or more hours). The hour thresholds come from the source’s Light attributes. Dappled/filtered light is represented by indirect light; explicit partial-sun/shade descriptions permit the partial-sun choice, with protection from afternoon sun where advised. This is not an assertion that indoor and outdoor light are interchangeable. Plants with explicitly documented low-light tolerance accept that choice too. Bright indirect light may still be preferable, and variegated cultivars may need more light. Plants needing afternoon shade retain that warning in their notes. The app does not infer exact lux levels or optimum flowering conditions.

Lemon balm's source allows moist to dry soil, so all three ordinary moisture options are accepted. Other profiles represent a broad growing condition rather than every tolerable alternative. Pot size, medium, drainage, season, cultivar and dormancy affect care. Herbs and garden shrubs may need outdoor conditions; a bright room is not equivalent to full sun. Most profiles describe active growth; the species notes cover relevant dormancy exceptions.

## Score

50 points for a watering condition that matches the simplified profile, plus 50 for supported light. These are editorial weights: **0%, 50% or 100% is a playful conditions-match score, not a survival probability or horticultural measurement**. The app states this in both languages and in shared text.

Temperature remains a visible contextual input and is saved in history, but is not scored. The former exact per-plant temperature targets were unsupported and have been removed. Seasonal temperature requirements cannot be faithfully reduced to one ideal value without additional questions; no temperature targets were invented. Relevant qualitative advice (cool conditions, frost protection, drafts) is provided from the species references.

## Corrections made after consulting the sources

- Spider plant, corn plant, heartleaf philodendron, Pilea, Rex begonia and Fatsia use a moist profile rather than the earlier assumed surface-dry profile.
- Pothos, wax plant and dwarf umbrella tree are allowed to dry between waterings, as their descriptions specify.
- Florist's cyclamen is watered when the soil feels dry, avoiding the crown, with reduced watering during summer dormancy.
- Florist's kalanchoe prefers bright indirect light and may scorch under prolonged direct sun.
- Guzmania lingulata keeps water in its central cup; the form shows that option only for this plant and notes sparse root watering.
- Source redirects and catalog names are saved explicitly, rather than guessing URLs from botanical names.

## History and language

`plantsLanguage` stores `fr` or `en`; browser language is used only when there is no valid saved preference. New `plantHistory` records use stable plant IDs and `schemaVersion: 2`, with `kind: conditions-match`. Legacy survival-score entries remain untouched and are not reinterpreted. Storage failures do not block use. No history UI or tracking service is introduced.
